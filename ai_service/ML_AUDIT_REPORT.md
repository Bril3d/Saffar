## 2. Dataset Audit & Provenance

Neither raw nor pre-processed datasets were committed to version control (`ai_service/data/` was unpopulated). All data is generated deterministically via `scripts/generate_data.py` using `random.seed(42)`.

```
               ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
               │           generate_data.py (random.seed(42))           │
               └───────┬───────────────────┬───────────────────┬────────┘
                       │                   │                   │
                       ▼                   ▼                   ▼
                vet_data.csv         farm_data.csv       demand_data.csv
                 (205 rows)           (105 rows)           (4,368 rows)
                200 Normal           100 Normal           12 Governorates
                5 Injected Outliers  5 Injected Outliers  52 Weeks × 7 Drugs
```

### Dataset Specifications

### 1. Veterinary Dataset (`vet_data.csv`)
- **Size:** 205 records $\times$ 7 columns (`vet_id`, `prescriptions_per_month`, `unique_farms`, `avg_dosage`, `reserve_class_ratio`, `governorate`, `is_anomaly`).
- **Class Distribution:** 200 normal (97.56%), 5 outlier (2.44%).
- **Label Provenance:** **Synthetic rule injection**. Outliers are generated by shifting distributions:
  - Normal vets: `prescriptions_per_month` $\sim \mathcal{N}(28, 8)$, `reserve_class_ratio` $\sim \mathcal{U}(0, 0.08)$.
  - Outliers: `prescriptions_per_month` $\in [120, 180]$, `reserve_class_ratio` $\in [0.25, 0.50]$.

### 2. Farm Dataset (`farm_data.csv`)
- **Size:** 105 records $\times$ 6 columns (`farmer_id`, `declared_lot_size`, `sales_volume`, `volume_to_lot_ratio`, `governorate`, `is_anomaly`).
- **Class Distribution:** 100 normal (95.24%), 5 fraud (4.76%).
- **Label Provenance:** **Synthetic rule injection**. Fraudulent farms have sales volumes generated at $3\times$ to $5\times$ declared lot sizes vs normal $0.8\times$ to $1.2\times$.

### 3. Antibiotic Demand Dataset (`demand_data.csv`)
- **Size:** 4,368 records $\times$ 7 columns (`governorate`, `governorate_code`, `week`, `atc_code`, `aware_class`, `season`, `demand`).
- **Structure:** 12 Tunisian governorates $\times$ 52 weeks $\times$ 7 WHO AWaRe antibiotic classes.
- **Data Generation Function:** Deterministic mathematical curve:
  $$\text{demand} = \max\left(0, \left\lfloor \text{base} \cdot \left(1.0 + 0.3 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot \text{week}}{52}\right)\right) + \mathcal{N}(0, 10) \right\rfloor\right)$$
  *(Reserve-class drugs have base demand scaled down by 90%).*

## 3. Isolation Forest Anomaly Detection Audit

### Architecture & Training Implementation
- **Script:** `scripts/train_anomaly.py`
- **Serving Service:** `main.py`
- **Model Parameters:** `IsolationForest(contamination=0.05, random_state=42, n_estimators=100)`.
- **Pre-processing:** `StandardScaler()` fit on the full dataset.
- **Pre-existing Evaluation Code:** **Completely absent.** The script simply counts how many samples received a prediction of `-1` on the training set:
  ```python
  predictions = model.predict(X_scaled)
  anomalies = sum(1 for p in predictions if p == -1)
  print(f'[TRAIN] Vet anomaly model: {anomalies} anomalies detected out of {len(df)}')
  ```

### Label Reliability Assessment
> **Finding: Reliable Ground-Truth Labels DO NOT Exist.**  
> The `is_anomaly` column is an artificial flag created by the mock data generator script. In `train_anomaly.py`, the algorithm is trained completely unsupervised without ever reading `is_anomaly`. No clinical audits, vet board disciplinary records, or real abattoir inspection records exist in the repository.

### Empirical Measurements (Benchmarked Against Injected Synthetic Outliers)

| Model / Target | Dataset Size | Injected Positives | True Positives (TP) | False Positives (FP) | Precision | Recall | F1 Score | False Positive Rate (FPR) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Vet Anomaly** | 205 | 5 | 5 | 6 | **0.4545** | **1.0000** | **0.6250** | **0.0300 (3.0%)** |
| **Farm Anomaly** | 105 | 5 | 5 | 1 | **0.8333** | **1.0000** | **0.9091** | **0.0100 (1.0%)** |

#### Why Precision is Capped at 45.4% on Vets:
The training script hardcodes `contamination=0.05`. On 205 samples, $205 \times 0.05 \approx 10.25$, forcing Isolation Forest to flag exactly 11 samples as anomalies. Because there are only 5 real injected outliers, the model is mathematically compelled to produce at least 6 false positives, artificially depressing precision to $\frac{5}{11} \approx 45.45\%$.

## 4. XGBoost Demand Forecasting Audit

### Architecture & Training Implementation
- **Script:** `scripts/train_forecast.py`
- **Serving Service:** `main.py`
- **Model Parameters:** `XGBRegressor(n_estimators=100, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.1, random_state=42)`.
- **Features Used:** `['governorate_code', 'week', 'atc_encoded', 'season']`
- **Target:** `'demand'`
- **Pre-existing Evaluation Code:** **None.** Only prints sample predictions from the top 5 training rows (`preds = model.predict(sample)`). No MAE, RMSE, MAPE, or baseline comparisons were recorded.

### Critical Methodological Limitations & Leakage
1. **Zero Temporal Validation:** The original script fits on all 52 weeks simultaneously.
2. **Lack of Lag / Autoregressive Structure:** The model does not use historical lags ($y_{t-1}, y_{t-4}$), rolling averages, or seasonal moving windows.
3. **Extrapolation Failure of Decision Trees:** In `main.py (lines 136–138)`, the API predicts `next_week = current_week + 1`. Decision trees cannot extrapolate trend dynamics beyond their bounded partition space (week 53 will simply evaluate to the leaf for week 52).

### Empirical Evaluation: In-Sample vs. Temporal Holdout vs. Baselines
To establish measured baselines, an out-of-time evaluation split was executed:
- **Train Split:** Weeks 1–40 (3,360 records)
- **Temporal Test Split:** Weeks 41–52 (1,008 records) — *Final quarter holdout*

| Model / Baseline | Evaluation Strategy | MAE | RMSE | $R^2$ Score | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Existing Saved Model** | In-Sample (All 52 Weeks) | 29.37 | 36.97 | 0.6426 | **Overfitted / Severe Leakage** (trained & tested on same data) |
| **Global Mean Baseline** | In-Sample Mean ($\bar{y} = 107.03$) | 50.36 | 61.85 | 0.0000 | Static global average |
| **Temporal XGBoost** | **Holdout (Weeks 41–52)** | **31.60** | **39.89** | **0.3336** | **Trained strictly on past (Weeks 1–40)** |
| **Baseline 1: Historical Group Mean** | Holdout (Weeks 41–52) | 34.90 | 44.47 | 0.1718 | Mean demand grouped by `(governorate, drug)` |
| **Baseline 2: Lag-1 Persistence** | Holdout (Weeks 41–52) | 38.36 | 49.60 | -0.0305 | Repeats week 40 demand for all future weeks |
| **Baseline 3: Train Global Mean** | Holdout (Weeks 41–52) | 43.86 | 54.22 | -0.2307 | Global mean of weeks 1–40 |

#### Empirical Baseline Comparison Takeaway:
On the temporal holdout set, **XGBoost achieves an MAE of 31.60**, outperforming:
- The **Historical Group Mean baseline** (MAE 34.90) by **9.45%** ($+3.30$ MAE improvement).
- The **Last-Observation Persistence baseline** (MAE 38.36) by **17.62%** ($+6.76$ MAE improvement).
- The **Naive Global Mean baseline** (MAE 43.86) by **27.95%** ($+12.26$ MAE improvement).

## 6. Exact Verification & Metric Calculation Commands

To reproduce the exact audit metrics on any clean machine, run the following commands:

### Step 1: Install Dependencies
```powershell
pip install scikit-learn==1.5.0 pandas==2.2.0 numpy==1.26.4 xgboost==2.1.0 joblib==1.4.2
```

### Step 2: Generate Datasets & Train Pipeline
```powershell
cd ai_service
python scripts/generate_data.py
python scripts/train_anomaly.py
python scripts/train_forecast.py
```

### Step 3: Run the Complete Audit & Verification Script
Execute the evaluation harness:
```powershell
python -c "
import os, pandas as pd, numpy as np, joblib
from sklearn.metrics import precision_score, recall_score, f1_score, confusion_matrix, mean_absolute_error, mean_squared_error, r2_score
from xgboost import XGBRegressor

data_dir = 'data'
models_dir = 'models'

# 1. Vet Anomaly
v_df = pd.read_csv(f'{data_dir}/vet_data.csv')
v_m = joblib.load(f'{models_dir}/vet_anomaly.joblib')
v_s = joblib.load(f'{models_dir}/vet_scaler.joblib')
X_v = v_s.transform(v_df[['prescriptions_per_month', 'unique_farms', 'avg_dosage', 'reserve_class_ratio']].values)
v_pred = (v_m.predict(X_v) == -1).astype(int)
v_true = v_df['is_anomaly'].values
tn, fp, fn, tp = confusion_matrix(v_true, v_pred).ravel()
print(f'[VET ANOMALY] P: {precision_score(v_true, v_pred):.4f} | R: {recall_score(v_true, v_pred):.4f} | F1: {f1_score(v_true, v_pred):.4f} | FPR: {fp/(fp+tn):.4f}')

# 2. Demand Forecasting (Temporal Validation)
d_df = pd.read_csv(f'{data_dir}/demand_data.csv')
enc = joblib.load(f'{models_dir}/atc_encoder.joblib')
d_df['atc_encoded'] = enc.transform(d_df['atc_code'])
feats = ['governorate_code', 'week', 'atc_encoded', 'season']

train = d_df[d_df['week'] <= 40]
test = d_df[d_df['week'] > 40]

xgb = XGBRegressor(n_estimators=100, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.1, random_state=42)
xgb.fit(train[feats].values, train['demand'].values)
preds = xgb.predict(test[feats].values)
y_test = test['demand'].values

print(f'[FORECAST XGB] MAE: {mean_absolute_error(y_test, preds):.2f} | RMSE: {np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_test, preds)):.2f} | R2: {r2_score(y_test, preds):.4f}')

# Baseline: Historical Group Mean
g_means = train.groupby(['governorate_code', 'atc_code'])['demand'].mean().to_dict()
base_preds = [g_means.get((r['governorate_code'], r['atc_code']), train['demand'].mean()) for _, r in test.iterrows()]
print(f'[BASELINE GROUP MEAN] MAE: {mean_absolute_error(y_test, base_preds):.2f} | RMSE: {np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_test, base_preds)):.2f}')
"
```
